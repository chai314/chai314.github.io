## The uniformisation theorem

<!-- AIWROTE -->
*Visiting Students' Research Programme, School of Mathematics, Tata Institute of Fundamental Research, Colaba, Mumbai. 20 May to 21 June 2024.*

<!-- AIWROTE -->
Nineteen students were selected nationally for the mathematics programme that year. Mine was a reading project that ended in a talk.

<!-- AIWROTE -->
{{< claim kind="Theorem" name="Klein, Koebe, Poincaré" >}}
Every simply connected Riemann surface is conformally equivalent to exactly one of the open unit disc \\(\mathbb{D}\\), the complex plane \\(\mathbb{C}\\), or the Riemann sphere \\(\hat{\mathbb{C}}\\).
{{< /claim >}}

{{< surfaces >}}

<!-- AIWROTE -->
{{< mn tilt="1.5" >}}Klein is said to have thought of it at 2:30 am, mid asthma attack.{{< /mn >}}

<!-- AIWROTE -->
The route I followed goes through potential theory, after the last chapter of Gamelin's *Complex Analysis*.

{{< steps >}}
<!-- AIWROTE -->
{{< step title="Subharmonic functions on a Riemann surface" >}}
Harmonic and subharmonic functions make sense chart by chart, and the maximum principle survives the move from the plane.
{{< /step >}}
<!-- AIWROTE -->
{{< step title="Perron's method" >}}
Take the supremum of a well-chosen family of subharmonic functions. The result is either harmonic or identically \\(+\infty\\).
{{< /step >}}
<!-- AIWROTE -->
{{< step title="Green's functions" >}}
Apply Perron's method to functions with a logarithmic pole. If a Green's function exists, the surface maps conformally onto the disc, by way of the Riemann mapping theorem.
{{< /step >}}
<!-- AIWROTE -->
{{< step title="Bipolar Green's functions" >}}
These exist on every Riemann surface. When there is no Green's function, they give a conformal map onto the plane or the sphere instead.
{{< /step >}}
{{< /steps >}}
